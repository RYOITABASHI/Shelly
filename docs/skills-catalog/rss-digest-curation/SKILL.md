---
name: rss-digest-curation
description: Curate a list of feeds/sources into a skimmable digest by relevance, instead of forwarding every new item as noise.
---
For an agent that periodically pulls from RSS/news/blog sources and
reports back, curation quality matters more than coverage — a digest that
includes everything is equivalent to no digest at all:

1. **Filter by relevance to the stated topic before anything else.** Define
   what this digest is actually for (a narrow topic, not "anything new")
   and drop items that only loosely match, even if the source itself is
   generally on-topic.
2. **Deduplicate across sources.** Multiple outlets often cover the same
   story — collapse them into one item citing the best source, rather than
   listing near-identical headlines three times.
3. **Prioritize substance over volume.** A source that posts 20 times a day
   shouldn't dominate the digest just by publishing more — weigh items by
   how much they matter to the stated topic, not by how many a source
   happened to produce this period.
4. **Summarize, don't just re-title.** A digest item should say what's
   actually new or notable in a sentence, not just restate the headline —
   the reader is deciding whether to click through, and a headline alone
   often doesn't tell them that.
5. **Track what's already been reported** so a recurring digest doesn't
   re-surface the same story across multiple runs just because the source
   updated the article or it's still trending.
6. **Keep a fixed cadence and length.** A digest that's sometimes 3 items
   and sometimes 40 is harder to build a habit of reading than one that's
   reliably a consistent, bounded size — cut to the top items by relevance
   rather than including everything that cleared the topic filter.
